What improved. Phase-one cyst pops went from 7 to 1, and the deaths they caused from 34 to 0. Intermissions went from 3 clean out of 9 to 10 out of 14. Crosswinds launches a player could have cancelled alone went from 23 in 23 sets to 16 in 32, tornado hits per Tempest fell from 9.2 to 6.7, and deaths a minute fell from 5.8 to 3.4.

What did not. The Serpent's Fury enrage ended 2 of the last 13 pulls, and Mutilate deaths rose to 32 in the last third (9 of them on pull 28 alone). Tornado deaths held level (16, 17, 19) even as hits per Tempest fell; the later pulls saw more Tempests.

How Mythic Sszorak runs

Read off this log rather than a guide: every pull ran to the same clock. Each phase one has two Apex Predator combos, and each combo is followed by a Serpent's Fury pop, a Venomous Surge and a Crosswinds set. Dig In then opens a 25-second intermission, and the cycle repeats.

Apex PredatorFive tank casts in a random order: two Ravage, two Mutilate, one Tempest. Ravage is a frontal on the tank; anyone else in it takes the hit (10 times this night, killing 6). Mutilate is a frontal the raid splits into two soak groups.
TempestTornadoes come out of the boss and roam the room for most of the next minute. 50% of the night's tornado hits came in the first ten seconds after the cast and 47% more than fifteen seconds after it, while the raid was busy with Surge and Crosswinds.
Serpent's FuryOne player is marked. Sszorak builds rage until fourteen more stand in the circle with them, 15 in all, a hard minimum; then he charges the mark (To the Slaughter) and everyone in it gets Virulence, which bursts in lines five seconds later. At 100 rage he gains Unbound Ferocity, and the pull is over.
Venomous Surge & Viscous CystTwo players are marked for ten seconds and drop a cyst where they stand. Touching a cyst pops it: a big knockback and a damage-over-time on everyone near it. The cysts are for the intermission.
Raging CrosswindsEight players get an arrow and are launched along it about eight seconds later. Two players with opposite arrows who meet cancel each other. Every class except Paladins and Priests can also cancel their own launch with a movement ability.
Dig InThe intermission. Winds sweep the platform in three gusts; the raid pops a cyst before each one at about 3.5, 12.2 and 20.5 seconds in, so the knockback carries everyone into the wind instead of off the edge.

Tornadoes

685 tornado hits across 98 Tempests, counting a tornado tagging the same player twice within a second as one hit. They killed 52, and they were the first death of a pull more often than anything else: tornadoes (13), virulence (6) and mutilate (6).

Per Tempest the raid took 9.2 hits in pulls 1–13, 5.7 in 14–25 and 6.7 in 26–38. Per Tempest the player was alive for, the non-tanks hit most often were Valanarr (0.65), Atheniás (0.50), Shazàm (0.49) and Шамшыммыч (0.49), against a median of 0.30. The tanks stand where the tornadoes come out of the boss: Laradendusk (0.90) and Vesemirr (0.36).

Cysts in phase one

A cyst popped outside the intermission is an accident: everyone near it is knocked across the room, and at the edge that means off it. 9 went off that way, on pulls 2, 5, 8, 10, 12, 19 and 35, catching 18 players at the median. 7 of them went off within ten seconds of being dropped, which means they were dropped where the raid stood. They killed 37, by knocking people off the platform or by the damage they leave behind.

This is the clearest improvement of the night. After pull 25 only one more went off (pull 35), and it killed nobody.

The intermission

35 intermissions were opened across the night and 21 went cleanly: three cysts, about eight and a half seconds apart, and at most one death. They cost 75 deaths in all (22% of every death before a wipe).

The failures come in two kinds. Two cysts within two seconds, which wastes one, so a later gust has nothing to ride: 5 times (pulls 4, 16 (intermission 3), 22, 26 and 34), 41 dead. A pop missing or late: 6 times (pulls 6, 8, 10, 15, 17 and 30), 53 dead. The last intermission to lose five or more players was on pull 34.

The Fury holder must not set off the pop while Dig In is being cast: on pull 6 it went off at 100.0s, as Dig In started, and Virulence killed 11 that pull.

Raging Crosswinds

661 knock-ups across 84 sets. 488 were cancelled by a partner, and 94 by the player's own movement within a second and a half of the launch: mostly Alfi (26), Huibalast (26), Abeei (19), Squashiclysm (6) and Duruwarr (5). 59 launches went off on a player who could have cancelled alone: 34 pressed movement too late, and 25 pressed none. 20 more were Paladins and Priests, who can only be caught by a partner: Ghani (6 of 30), Shazàm (6 of 43), Indris (4 of 41), Aelyssar (2 of 41), Aeonara (2 of 37) and Angryde (0 of 42).

Launches that could have been avoided alone: 23 in 23 sets in pulls 1–13, 20 in 29 in 14–25, 16 in 32 in 26–38. Most often launched with a cancel in their kit: Mattiseppo (14 of 41: 8 late, 6 with no movement), Alfi (7 of 42: 7 late, 0 with no movement), Atheniás (7 of 35: 6 late, 1 with no movement) and Duruwarr (6 of 37: 3 late, 3 with no movement). Crosswinds killed 23; 3 of them went off the edge, and the rest died to the launch damage.

The Serpent's Fury clock

The pop needs 15 bodies in the circle, the marked player included, so a raid with 14 alive and no battle res ready cannot pop at all. 11 pulls reached Unbound Ferocity. In 5 of them (9, 11, 21, 29 and 31) the raid was already under 15 when it came, so the deaths ended those pulls, not the clock. The other 6 (3, 18, 22, 24, 27 and 36) still had the numbers, and the pop came too late. Every pop that did go off had 15 in the circle: 16 players at the median by Virulence and never fewer than 14. On the two pops with only fourteen players, the marked player was Piiaan, whose Beast Mastery pet stands in but never gets the debuff.

The boss reaches 100 rage 57.7 seconds after a pop within a phase, and 82.3 seconds after the last pop before an intermission, because the rage keeps building through Dig In. The first pop after the intermission cannot go off until the post-intermission combo ends; when it worked it went off at 154.6 seconds at the median. So the second pop of the pull sets the deadline for the third. Pop two landed anywhere from 72.6 to 82.6 seconds this night, and every second earlier is a second less after the combo.

Of the timing failures, 4 pulls died at that first post-intermission pop (3, 18, 24 and 27). 1 died at the next one (22), and 1 after the second intermission (36): pull 16: pop four at 202.6s, so the boss would enrage at 284.9s, and the raid popped at 282.4s; pull 29: pop four at 202.3s, so the boss would enrage at 284.6s, and it did at 285.0s, but the raid had been under 15 alive since 220.1s; pull 36: pop four at 200.7s, so the boss would enrage at 283.0s, and it did at 282.9s.

Against the plan (every dps and both tanks in, healers only to cover a dead one), 54 of 89 pops had every alive dps and tank in the circle. Missing most often while alive: Atheniás (8), Abeei (7), Alfi (6), Huibalast (6) and Squashiclysm (4). Healers stood in when no dps or tank was dead on 36 pops: Shazàm (28 of 39), Valanarr (16 of 31), Ghani (5 of 12) and Aeonara (2 of 8). A healer who soaks takes a Virulence they did not need, which is one more set of lines in the raid. Second-hand Virulence, someone clipped by a line, fell from 1.9 per pop to 1.1; it killed 47 across the night.

Mutilate, Ravage and the floor

Mutilate killed 56, mostly through the Mutilated Gash bleed after an under-filled soak. Pull 28 was the worst at 9, with 10 of its soakers still carrying Gash from the soak before (14 such soaks across the whole night). Mutilate deaths rose from 13 in the first third to 32 in the last. Ravage hit someone other than a tank 10 times and killed 6. Venomous Surge killed 10, and the caustic puddles 15.

Deaths

346 deaths before a pull was over. Deaths after the raid fell under 15 alive with no battle res ready are not counted (three seconds of grace, so one blow killing several counts in full). Neither are deaths after a jump-off reset (1 pulls reset that way before going under) or after an enrage. That leaves out 315 and 87 more. The biggest killers were intermission (winds and cysts) (75), mutilate (56) and tornadoes (52).

How this is read. The platform's edge is about 60 yards from the middle. No player still alive fifteen seconds later was ever logged past 69. A death whose last eight seconds went past 65 yards, with no killing blow, is a fall. It is blamed on what moved the player there in the seven seconds before they left the platform: a Crosswinds launch, a cyst pop, the intermission's winds, or nothing. Nothing means they walked or jumped off. Three of those inside ten seconds is a called wipe. Under the number is fewer than 15 alive, counting a ready battle res as one more. Charges follow the raid rule: one at the pull and one every 4.5 minutes with twenty players, spent by Rebirth or Intercession; a shaman's Reincarnation is free. Every death more than three seconds after either point is left out. A tornado hit is a Tempest hit without the tick flag. A cyst pop is a burst of Viscous Cyst hits without the tick flag; pops during Dig In or in the ten seconds after it are planned, all others are accidents. A Fury pop is the mark dropping with Virulence going out to the soakers within 1.5 seconds; the rage windows are read off the enrages themselves. A Crosswinds launch is Turbulent Gusts lasting past 1.5 seconds. A solo cancel is the player's own movement ability (Alter Time, Blink, Burning Rush, Charge, Chi Torpedo, Demonic Circle: Teleport, Disengage, Fel Rush, Feral Lunge, Glide, Gust of Wind, Heroic Leap, Intervene, Roll, Shadowstep, Shield Charge, Shimmer, Vengeful Retreat, Wild Charge) cast from half a second before to 1.5 seconds after the launch. A launch with that movement pressed later counts as late.
